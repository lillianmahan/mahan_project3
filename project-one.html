Riso Skirt

A wearable art project combining photography, risographic printing, and textile construction to explore material experimentation and visual storytelling.

Overview

Riso Skirt is a mixed‑media project that transforms photographic imagery into wearable art using risograph printing techniques applied directly to fabric. The piece investigates how print processes can exist beyond paper and interact with the body.

The Challenge

The goal was to create a sewn garment that incorporated photography through risograph printing while experimenting with unconventional materials and processes. Translating a traditionally rigid print technique onto fabric posed both technical and conceptual challenges.

Role & Tools

  • Role: Creator and Designer
  • Tools used: Camera, Risograph Machine, Transfer Paper, Iron, and a Sewing Machine.

The Solution

I printed my photographic images using a risograph machine and transferred the prints onto fabric, which was then constructed into a skirt. This process merged image‑making with garment construction, resulting in a functional and conceptual wearable artwork.

Results

The piece was exhibited in a juried show, where it engaged viewers by disrupting expectations of both print and fashion. Audience members responded to the unfamiliar materials and the tension between craft and experimentation.

Reflection

Through this project, I learned that risograph printing can successfully be applied to fabric with careful experimentation. I also discovered that viewers are deeply drawn to work that feels unexpected, tactile, and intentionally unconventional.
